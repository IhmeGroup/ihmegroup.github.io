  <div class="research-detail-top__text">
    <p>
      The combustors that will power the next generation of aircraft, rockets, and power systems rely on combustion phenomena that are difficult to measure and that couple across disparate scales. Our work spans both experiment and high-fidelity simulation, letting us understand how turbulence, chemistry, shocks, and heat release interact under extreme conditions to shape system-level performance, stability, and emissions.
    </p>
    <p>
      Some of our explorationsin this area include:
      <ul>
        <li><ins>Turbulent flames and ignition:</ins> Using high-fidelity simulation to study how turbulent flames ignite, stabilize, and couple with sprays, evaporation, and heat transfer across a wide range of fuels and operating conditions.</li>
        <li><ins>Predictive combustion modeling:</ins> Developing fidelity-adaptive, radiation, and reduced-order models that make high-fidelity simulation of complex reacting flows both affordable and reliable.</li>
        <li><ins>Advanced propulsion architectures and fuels:</ins> Studying ignition, mixing, and stability in liquid rockets, rotating detonation engines, scramjets, and porous-media burners for carbon-free fuels such as ammonia and hydrogen blends.</li>
        <li><ins>Combustion instabilities and noise:</ins> Characterizing how unsteady heat release couples with combustor acoustics to generate thermoacoustic instabilities and both direct and indirect combustion noise.</li>
      </ul>
    </p>
  </div>

    <h3>Alternative Fuels and Porous Media</h3>
    <p>
      Carbon-free fuels such as ammonia burn poorly in conventional designs, with narrow flammability and elevated emissions. We develop novel porous-media architectures that recirculate heat within a solid matrix to stabilize lean ammonia-hydrogen flames, and use pore-resolved simulations to reveal the coupling between chemistry and the solid matrix.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="research-detail-card">
    <img src="/assets/img/detfig.png" alt="Simulated structure of a detonation wave propagating through a stratified, product-diluted mixture">
    <h3>Detonations and High-Speed Flows</h3>
    <p>
      Detonation-based engines and scramjets promise higher efficiency than traditional designs but depend on highly coupled and compressible flow physics. Using high-fidelity simulation, we characterize these phenomena to improve prediction and inform the design of reliable system configurations.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="research-detail-card">
    <img src="/assets/img/acost1.png" alt="Simulation of a non-premixed flame responding to a transverse acoustic mode in a combustor">
    <h3>Aeroacoustical Instabilities</h3>
    <p>
      Unsteady heat release coupled with combustor acoustics can drive instabilities that limit engine performance and durability. By studying the direct and indirect responses of combustors to acoustical perturbation, the effects of combustion and model design choice on aeroacoustical prediction can be quantified.
    </p>
  </div>
